# Prior work on the Alon–Jaeger–Tarsi conjecture

Literature checked through 27 November 2026. The comparison concerns the homogeneous existence statement for every invertible matrix in a stated dimension range.

The appropriate comparison is a two-dimension advance over TCSExam's earlier Lean result, with a separate formalization of the existing prime-dependent range for p ≥ 7. The dimension-ten proof uses ordinary symmetrized weight tables and a MacWilliams identity. The dimension-eleven proof normalizes a hypothetical counterexample, refines the tables with marks, and applies a finite exact contradiction certificate to the resulting identities. TCSExam's preceding proof also uses Fourier-related weight tables and an exact certificate, with column-pair constraints. The difference is the refinement and the dimensions it closes. ## Other field sizes, covering results, and remaining scope

- **Proper prime powers.** Alon–Tarsi, *A nowhere-zero point in linear mappings*, Combinatorica 9 (1989), Theorem 1, proves the conjecture for every q = pᵏ, k ≥ 2, in arbitrary dimension. Jafari, arXiv:2608.23647v2, 17 September 2026, Theorem 1.1, treats r simultaneous invertible mappings (including the identity) when k ≥ r. Its rank-four covering discussion concerns blockers of rank at most four. [Alon–Tarsi, original paper](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/Publications/A%20nowhere-zero%20point%20in%20linear%20mappings.pdf) · [Jafari, current preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23647v2)
- **Large primes and covering.** Nagy–Pach, JEMS, published online first on 11 April 2025, Theorem A (arXiv v1, Theorem 1(ii)), proves arbitrary dimension for primes p > 61 with p ≠ 79, allowing one independently prescribed forbidden value per input and output coordinate. Nagy–Pach–Tomon, *Irredundant hyperplane covers*, §§1 and 6, relates AJT to affine hyperplane covers. Their Theorem 1.7 permits any fixed number of invertible maps over sufficiently large fields, and Lemma 6.1 gives a sufficient irredundant-cover bound. Neither result supplies a guarantee for arbitrary 12 × 12 matrices over 𝔽₅. [Nagy–Pach, journal](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/14298707) · [Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.03956v1#S6) · [Nagy–Pach–Tomon, full text](https://cs.bme.hu/~ppp/publications/Hyperplane_covers.pdf#page=11)
- **Recent group-ring reduction.** Nagy–Pach, arXiv:2604.26320v1, 29 April 2026, Theorem 1, proves that their Conjecture 2 on lifting a modular group-ring vanishing identity to an integral one would imply AJT for every prime p > 3. The lifting is the outstanding hypothesis. [Preprint, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26320v1#S1)
- **Recent permanent-rank papers.** Yu's *Part Two*, v1, 2 March 2026, develops further permanent-rank results. *Part Three*, v1, 14 February 2026, Theorem 3 and Corollary 4, claims a four-basis additive-basis result in characteristic three. Neither supplies an all-matrix AJT bound over 𝔽₅ above the earlier range. [Part Two](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202603.0035/v1) · [Part Three](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202602.1158.v1)
- **The lower field-size cutoff.** Over 𝔽₃, the invertible matrix with rows (1, 1) and (1, −1) gives an explicit counterexample in dimension two. For any input a, b ≠ 0, one has a = b or a = −b, so one output is zero. Block-diagonal padding gives counterexamples in every dimension n ≥ 2. This is outside the original q ≥ 4 hypothesis. [Alon–Tarsi, Conjecture 1 and the observation following it](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/Publications/A%20nowhere-zero%20point%20in%20linear%20mappings.pdf#page=1)

Equivalently, AJT asks whether two bases of linear forms can have their zero hyperplanes cover the whole space. Any use of general hyperplane-cover bounds must preserve the two-basis constraint or yield a bound strong enough to exclude such a cover. The new theorem is an existence result on a bounded dimension range. A polynomial-time search theorem for unbounded dimension, a bound on the number of witnesses, or a decision result for arbitrary matrices without invertibility would require a separate claim and evidence.
