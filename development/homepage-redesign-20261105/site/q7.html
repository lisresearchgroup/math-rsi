How every matching is covered

  1. E36 covers the dense range: every matching with at least 46 edges extends to a Hamilton cycle retaining all its edges.
  2. Double counting alone gives at least 45 edges in every inclusion-maximal matching. E38 excludes exactly 45, giving at least 46 edges; this maximality bound does not use the older dense-layer theorems.
  3. Add free cube edges to the original matching M until it is inclusion-maximal, obtaining N ⊇ M. E36 supplies a Hamilton cycle containing every edge of N, so it also contains every edge of M.

The key bridge is monotone completion: preserve the actual matching throughout, then use a global obstruction to small maximal matchings to enter the proven dense range.

Evidence and review status

The source project accepted the complete R7 working proof as sound-modulo-imports, same-family and provisional. External peer review is pending; there is no Lean formalization.

Two original C++ programs were compiled and run on Mac. E38 counts 35,360 legal single faces and reconstructs 18,237,120 ordered face pairs; zero pairs satisfy the directional necessary conditions for a maximal 45-edge matching. These are face/pair counts, not matching counts or coverage percentages.

This is a local E38 recomputation. Some large certificates from older layers are absent from the supplied package, and the complete imported proof chain was not replayed end to end.

Q₆ theorem and six-direction corollary

Every matching of Q₆ extends to a Hamilton cycle. For the at-most-six-direction consequence in arbitrary dimension Qₙ, the lifting input is the prescribed-endpoint P₆ path theorem, combined with published lifting results.
