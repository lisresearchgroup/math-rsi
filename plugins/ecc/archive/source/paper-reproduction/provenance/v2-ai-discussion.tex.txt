\subsection{A workflow with explicit acceptance conditions}
The research used an ARIS-style workflow: a persistent formal specification, linked literature and experiment records, parallel exploration, and separate review of proposed arguments. The specification fixed the unrestricted matrix quantifiers before search. A positive candidate required full rank and a complete nonzero-message distance check; a negative conclusion required a reduction covering the entire target class. The research memory retained unsuccessful routes with their actual scope, preventing a fixed-seed exclusion from becoming a global nonexistence claim in a later session.

The final proof does not require replaying the discovery trajectory. Candidate generation, constraint-programming models, prompts, and search order guided exploration. The accepted argument uses the reductions of Section~\ref{sec:reduction}, the published class bound, and the finite evidence of Section~\ref{sec:computation}. Figure~\ref{fig:flow} shows this dependency structure.



\subsection{Recorded interventions and their scope}
The project records support two concrete examples of model contribution. A Gemini consultation suggested testing native early-rejection filters. A profile of $2^{20}$ representatives of an earlier fixed subcode used 3,543,023 distance checks, or 3.378890 per candidate. This measurement motivated local implementation of bit-parallel low-bit masks. The implemented mask algorithm and its full-domain justification were developed in the local research process; the model's performance prediction was revised by measurement. The profile concerns a length-43, dimension-eight subcode and a 35-dimensional quotient; it is not a timing result for the final 172-case workload. Appendix~\ref{app:attribution} records the intervention and the corrections to the model suggestions.

A Grok review sharpened the missing completeness check. At that stage, independent validation established that saved cosets satisfied the constraints, while the review packet already noted that an independent full scan was missing. The review explicitly required a second implementation to decide acceptance or rejection over the entire quotient and compare complete output sets. The project then implemented the opposite-pivot, Gray-order scan described above. This intervention has a concrete consequence: it tests omitted admissible cosets, which member validation alone cannot test.

A suggested completion family was also rejected by a Griesmer contradiction before enumeration (Appendix~\ref{app:attribution}, A3). The recorded interventions include both useful advice and corrected proposals. Local agents worked on source retrieval, structural deductions, mother reconstruction, code, and proof exposition. Later consultations checked equations, quantifiers, classification scope, and evidence connections. These are documented research and review contributions. The final verification procedures operate on ordinary mathematical and computational objects and require no model API.

\subsection{What the Lean developments establish}
Both formal projects use Lean 4.34.1 and pinned mathlib. The first formalizes parity extension, puncturing, shortening, the Griesmer argument, the MacWilliams calculation, the full-support mother reduction, quotient normalization, classification coverage, and the corollaries. In the first project, all 172 mother matrices and the lower-bound generators for the $(43,10)$, $(44,10)$, and $(44,11)$ entries are connected to the abstract definitions and checked by kernel evaluation. The $[42,16,12]$ lower witness is checked by exhaustive Python enumeration; its separate Lean project formalizes the upper-bound reduction.

The final main theorem takes four explicit parameters: E1; E2; pairwise coordinate inequivalence of the catalogue; and pair incompatibility for each catalogue member. Its type therefore records the boundary between kernel-checked deduction and the published or external computational facts. A finer interface expands the scan and certificate assumptions. The second project proves the entire reduction to exact $[41,14,14]$ and takes E3 as an explicit parameter.

The audited theorem dependencies contain the usual Lean axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}, with no \texttt{sorryAx}. Explicit parameters do not appear in this axiom list. Consequently, a clean axiom audit verifies the deductions but does not discharge E1--E3, catalogue inequivalence, or the exhaustive computations. Appendix~\ref{app:formal} gives the exact interfaces.

\paragraph{A1: profiling suggestion and measurement.}
The recorded Gemini consultation suggested a native early-rejection profile. The tested quotient was the earlier dimension-35 one, not the final even dimension-32 quotient. Its $2^{20}$ candidates required 3,543,023 constraint checks; the predicted average of at most 2.3 checks was replaced by the measured 3.378890. The result informed the local implementation of six-bit masks. The provenance synopsis identifies the original profile and reconciliation records.

\paragraph{A2: enforcing an outstanding completeness check.}
The round-seven review packet already identified the missing independent full-domain check. Grok explicitly requested that another implementation decide every acceptance and rejection and compare the resulting sets. The subsequent implementation changed the pivot order, quotient basis, enumeration order, and distance representation. This attribution concerns the verification requirement and its execution, rather than claiming that the reviewer first discovered the omission.

\paragraph{A3: a rejected search proposal.}
An earlier Grok consultation proposed a completion family of the form $\{0\}\cup(b+V)$ with $\dim V=4$. The local mathematical check derived a binary $[26,5,\ge16]$ restriction, contradicting $g_2(5,16)=31>26$, and discarded the proposal before enumeration. The positive interventions above are thus selected examples from a process that also rejected model suggestions. They are not a complete interaction dataset or an estimate of model success rate.
