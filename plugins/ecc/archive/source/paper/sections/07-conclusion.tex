\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have shown that no binary linear $\codege{43}{10}{17}$ code exists, and derived exact values of $\dopt(n,k)$ at $(43,10)$, $(44,10)$, $(44,11)$, and $(42,16)$. The MacWilliams identities force a codeword of weight four in the dual code, which places a full-support $[40,7,18]$ subcode inside every hypothetical code; a complete enumeration then shows that no two distinct admissible even cosets of any of the 172 classified subcodes are compatible. This obstruction is stronger than needed: the triple-sum condition in \eqref{eq:extension} is never used. The published classifications, catalogue inequivalence, and the enumeration remain explicit inputs, both in the Lean development and in the computation.

\vspace{8pt}
The case $(45,11)$ remains open: Corollary~\ref{cor:three} excludes distance 18, but our arguments say nothing about a $[45,11,17]$ code. Parity extension of such a code would give an even $\codege{46}{11}{18}$ code; we have not studied whether a reduction of the type used here applies to it.
