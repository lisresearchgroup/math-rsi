\begin{abstract}
We prove that a binary linear code of length 43, dimension 10, and minimum distance at least 17 does not exist. The MacWilliams identities force a codeword of weight four in the dual code, and shortening on its support reduces the problem to extensions of the 172 classified full-support $[40,7,18]$ codes. For each such code, a complete enumeration of its $2^{32}$ even cosets shows that any two distinct cosets at distance at least 16 have a sum at distance below 16, so the required extension does not exist. Two independent enumerations and an independent replay of all 619,053,789 pair rejections verify this finite step. Puncturing and shortening then exclude $\codege{44}{10}{18}$, $\codege{44}{11}{17}$, and $\codege{45}{11}{18}$ codes, and a reduction to the published nonexistence of $[41,14,14]$ codes excludes $\codege{42}{16}{13}$. Together with explicit constructions, these results determine four values of $d_2(n,k)$ for which only bounds have been recorded since at least 1985, and give $16\le d_2(45,11)\le17$.
\end{abstract}
